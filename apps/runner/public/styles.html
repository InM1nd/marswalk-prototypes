Runner — style directions

Nine visual directions for the same endless-runner mechanic, drawn as a portrait phone frame (9:16): the format the game is actually played in from a social ad. Every sketch shows the same moment: the rider is mid-jump over a cone, a trail of coins is ahead, a tall barrier is coming up, and the HUD shows score, timer and combo. Physics, difficulty ramp, scoring and the prize/lead flow stay identical across all nine. Only the skin changes, driven by the campaign config.

Runner-specific notes (apply to every direction)

  • Portrait first. The current build is landscape: the world sits in the bottom 20% of the screen and the sky is empty. Each direction here puts the camera close, so the rider is about 15% of the screen height and the action is in the middle third.
  • Readability beats decoration. Obstacles must contrast with the background at a glance, both in silhouette and in value. Collectibles get the warmest or brightest color in the palette. The background is always the lowest-contrast layer.
  • Character per vertical. The rider and items are swappable per campaign: a scooter rider for mobility, a shopping cart and products for FMCG, a coin courier for banking. The hitboxes stay fixed, so new art never changes the difficulty.
  • Prize hook. In the target flow, a score threshold unlocks the prize, then the same win modal and lead form as the Wheel and the Scratch Card appear.
  • Effort. Effort is rated for procedural Pixi drawing (no sprite atlas). Pixel and Clay need baked sprites. Silhouette is the cheapest and the smoothest on low-end phones.

These are static concept sketches (SVG) for choosing a direction. The live demo has real physics, parallax, sound and the game-over flow. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts.
